New Research Funded by Elea Institute Shows Rapid Shift of U.S. Hospice System

National Analysis Finds Hospices Grew Since 2018 as For-Profit Ownership Expanded

CHICAGO — Elea Institute, a national leader in advancing access to high-quality hospice and palliative care, has funded research conducted by Center for Health Research, Policy & Strategy. The research fills a critical gap in current, publicly available data on the hospice market, examining the landscape from its national footprint, shift in ownership models and the growing role of private equity. The U.S. hospice market experienced a 46% growth in eight years.

"Elea Institute invested in this research to provide data that explicitly lays out what the current hospice landscape is," said Kent Mathy, Elea Institute's Board Chairman. "Our mission is to improve access to care while advancing research, and this research is a strong step towards ensuring we continue to meet that goal."

The change in the hospice landscape tells two different stories about the growth. The analysis finds that for-profit hospice care drove a large portion of the market's expansion, reaching 51.1% of the hospice landscape by the end of 2025, while the non-profit/government-operated hospices declined by 22.2%.

Among for-profit hospices with a documented change of ownership transactions (CHOW), more than 1 in 3 (38.7%) were associated with private equity funding. Because Medicare enrollment records do not require private equity disclosure, the full extent of private equity's role in hospice care remains unclear. The report calls for further research to understand how these ownership shifts are affecting the hospice system.

For the full report and executive summary, please visit eleainstitute.org.
